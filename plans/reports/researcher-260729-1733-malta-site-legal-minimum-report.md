# Malta Photography Website — Legal Requirements Minimum

**TLDR:** Privacy notice REQUIRED (GDPR); business identification + contact details REQUIRED (eCommerce Directive); price display rules apply IF VAT-registered. Terms & Conditions NOT required; cookie banner NOT required (no cookies/analytics). Article 11 VAT threshold is €35,000/year (2026).

## Research Scope

**Site profile:**
- Static one-page brochure (no accounts, login, payment, checkout, cart)
- Contact form: name, email, phone, location, services, message → WhatsApp (data to Meta)
- Fixed prices in EUR for photography/video/virtual-staging
- No cookies, no analytics, no tracking, no third-party embeds

## Question 1: Are Terms & Conditions Legally Required?

**Answer: NO — NOT required.**

**Reasoning:**
- Directive 2000/31/EC (eCommerce Directive) requires transparency and business identification but does **not** mandate a separate Terms & Conditions page.
- A static contact form does not constitute contract formation on the site; the contract forms later (after WhatsApp discussion and confirmation off-site).
- Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU requires pre-contractual information **at the beginning of the ordering process**, but this site has no ordering process.
- Malta's eCommerce regulations focus on transparency obligations (who you are, how to contact you, clear product descriptions, prices) — not contractual T&Cs.

**What IS required:** Business identification + contact details (see Question 4). Contract terms can be agreed in WhatsApp or via follow-up email, not on the site itself.

**What you can omit:** A separate Terms & Conditions page. You may optionally include a brief disclaimer (e.g., "By contacting us, you agree to our communication practices") but it is not legally mandatory.

## Question 2: Is a Privacy Notice Required?

**Answer: YES — REQUIRED under GDPR.**

**Reasoning:**
- The contact form collects personal data (name, email, phone, location, message).
- GDPR Articles 13–14 require that you provide a privacy notice (also called privacy policy or data protection notice) **at or before the point of collection**.
- Malta's IDPC (Information & Data Protection Commissioner) confirms in guidance that data collection requires transparency: the registration form must include a link to the privacy policy, **immediately visible** during data entry.

**Minimum content (GDPR + IDPC guidance):**

1. **Controller identification** — Your full legal name (sole trader name), contact address, email, phone.
2. **Purpose of processing** — "To respond to your photography/video service inquiry; to provide quotations and schedule consultations."
3. **Legal basis for processing** (Article 6 GDPR) — Choose ONE:
   - **(a) Consent** — "You consent to us processing your data to respond to your inquiry." (Explicit affirmative action, not pre-ticked)
   - **(f) Legitimate interest** — "We have a legitimate business interest in responding to inquiries and managing client relationships."
   - Recommendation: Use **(a) Consent** for clarity; add explicit checkbox: "I consent to processing my data to respond to this inquiry."
4. **Recipients of data** — "Your data will be shared with WhatsApp/Meta when you submit this form (data routed through Meta's systems)."
5. **Retention period** — E.g., "We retain your contact data for 1 year after your last interaction, then delete it. WhatsApp/Meta may retain data per their own policy."
6. **Data subject rights** — Brief summary: "You have the right to access, correct, delete, or object to processing of your data. Contact us at [email]."
7. **IDPC contact** — "If you believe your rights have been violated, contact the Information & Data Protection Commissioner: idpc.info@idpc.org.mt, +356 2328 7100, Floor 2, Airways House, High Street, Sliema SLM 1549, Malta."

**Presentation:** Link to privacy notice immediately visible on the form (e.g., "By submitting, you consent to our [Privacy Notice](link)"). Use clear, simple language; avoid legalese.

**Length:** Minimum 150–250 words. You are not required to draft a lengthy policy; a concise notice covering the 7 points above is sufficient.

**Not required in the privacy notice:**
- Elaborate data security measures (your responsibility but not mandatory disclosure detail)
- Detailed IDPC complaint procedures (just link to IDPC)
- Data processing agreements with WhatsApp (that's your responsibility; consumers don't need the legal contract)
- Sub-processor lists (not applicable for a simple contact form)

## Question 3: Is a Cookie Banner Required if No Cookies or Analytics?

**Answer: NO — NOT required.**

**Reasoning:**
- Malta's IDPC Guidance Note on Cookies Consent confirms: tracking cookies require **prior, informed consent**.
- If your site sets **zero cookies** (no analytics, no third-party tracking, no session cookies), there is no cookie banner requirement.
- If the site is self-contained HTML with no JavaScript, embeds, or tracking pixels, cookies do not apply.

**Exception:** If any third-party library or script is later added (e.g., Google Analytics, Hotjar, Facebook Pixel, Intercom, etc.), a cookie banner becomes mandatory.

**What you can omit:** Cookie banner, cookie policy (if truly no cookies exist). Once you add analytics or tracking, you must then add the banner and accompanying cookie policy.

## Question 4: Business Identification Requirements (eCommerce Directive 2000/31/EC)

**Answer: YES — REQUIRED. Minimum content:**

Under Directive 2000/31/EC (implemented in Malta) and Directive 2011/83/EU (Consumer Rights), online service providers must display:

1. **Legal name** — Your full name (sole trader registration name, e.g., "John Doe Photography").
2. **Contact address** — Physical address in Malta (e.g., "123 Main Street, Mellieħa, Malta").
3. **Contact email and/or phone** — A working email and/or phone number for customers to reach you. (This is mandatory to comply with the eCommerce requirement for accessible, transparent contact details.)
4. **Registration/identification details** (conditional):
   - If **VAT-registered**: Display your VAT number (e.g., "VAT: MT12345678").
   - If **self-employed/sole trader** but NOT VAT-registered: You may omit the VAT number. However, if you have a **trading name** registered with Jobsplus or equivalent, display that.
   - If you have a **business registration number**, display it. (In Malta, sole traders are typically registered with Jobsplus/Social Security and assigned a PE number; optional to display on the website but advisable for credibility.)

**How to present:** Include a footer or "About" section with these details, or a dedicated "Contact / Business Information" block.

**What you can omit:**
- Company registration number (only for limited companies, not sole traders).
- Detailed company incorporation details.
- Board of directors (not applicable to sole traders).

**Sole trader specifics:**
- As a self-employed photographer in Malta, you must register with Jobsplus as self-employed (no cost, a few days).
- You will receive a PE (Personal Employment) number and social security registration.
- You do **not** need to register as a limited company unless you choose to.
- Your "legal name" is your personal name (or a registered trading name if you've chosen one).

## Question 5: Price Display & VAT Requirements

**Answer: YES — Prices must comply with consumer protection law. Application depends on VAT status.**

### Current Article 11 Threshold (Malta 2026)

**VAT Article 11 domestic threshold: €35,000 annual turnover** (effective 2025–2026).

If your annual turnover stays below €35,000, you are eligible for VAT exemption under Article 11. This threshold applies to "small undertakings" and is mandatory to recalculate annually.

### If VAT-Registered

**Requirement:** Display prices **inclusive of all taxes** (gross price).
- EU law (Directive 2011/83/EU + Price Indication Directive) mandates that prices displayed to consumers include **all applicable taxes, including VAT**.
- Example: Instead of "€500 + VAT", display "€590 (incl. VAT 18%)".
- This applies whether you're VAT-registered voluntarily or mandatorily (turnover >€35k).

**Wording:** "Prices shown include VAT at 18%" or "€590 (incl. VAT)". Clear and explicit.

### If NOT VAT-Registered (Under €35,000 Threshold)

**Requirement:** Prices can be displayed **ex-VAT**, but **must be clearly labeled**.
- If you are not VAT-registered under Article 11, you do **not** charge VAT.
- You may display prices without VAT, but **must state clearly** that no VAT applies.
- Recommended wording: "€500 (VAT not applicable)" or "€500 ex-VAT (business not VAT-registered under Article 11)".
- Alternative: Display an inclusive total if you plan to invoice with a note "VAT not charged".

**Critical:** Confirm the exact wording requirement with a Maltese accountant before publishing, as consumer protection law expects clarity but the precise phrasing is not standardised in publicly available guidance. Malta's **Office for Fair Trading** (consumer protection authority) and your **accountant** should be consulted for final wording.

### Price Transparency for Services

EU Directive 2011/83/EU specifies that for distance contracts (like your website), the following must be **clear at the latest at the beginning of the ordering process**:
- Main characteristics of the service (photography, videography, virtual staging — you already describe this).
- **Total price** including all taxes and fees.
- Identity of the trader (business identification — covered in Q4).

Your site meets this: you display the services and fixed prices. If prices are fixed, no additional "ordering process" applies (customer contacts via WhatsApp to confirm).

**What you can omit:**
- Delivery costs (not applicable for a service-based business).
- Shipping disclaimers (no physical shipping).
- Price-match guarantees or dynamic pricing disclosures (you have fixed prices).
- 30-day price history for discounts (not applicable unless you offer discounted rates — even then, only if you advertise a discount).

## Question 6: Anything Else Genuinely Mandatory?

**Answer: Yes, one minor item — contract delivery and withdrawal right notice (conditional).**

### Contract Confirmation (eCommerce Directive + Consumer Rights)

If customers enter into a **contract on the site** (e.g., they complete an order and pay), you must provide written confirmation.

**Your case:** Since contracts are formed **off-site** (via WhatsApp or email after initial inquiry), this is **not applicable on the website itself**. However, once a customer commits via WhatsApp, you should send a written confirmation email or invoice stating the scope, price, and any terms.

**Not a website requirement, but a best practice:** Confirmation email to the customer after they book via WhatsApp.

### Consumer Right to Withdraw (Directive 2011/83/EU)

Distance contracts grant consumers a **14-day right to cancel/withdraw**.

**Your case:** This is **conditional**:
- If your contract is for **services** (photography/videography), withdrawal rights are typically **excluded or limited** once the service has commenced.
- If it's a **digital product** (virtual staging file delivery), withdrawal rights may apply but are often waived if the file is delivered.
- **Recommendation:** In your contract terms (sent via WhatsApp or email), state: *"Once the service has commenced or the digital file has been provided, the right to withdraw is excluded per Directive 2011/83/EU Article 16(m)."*

**On the website:** Not strictly required if no on-site contract forms. If you add an explicit booking/payment on the website later, you must then display cancellation/withdrawal terms **before checkout**.

**For now:** Not required, but mention in your WhatsApp confirmation or invoice.

## Unresolved Questions & Lawyer/Accountant Checkpoints

Before launching, confirm with qualified Maltese professionals:

1. **Exact VAT Article 11 status:** Is your anticipated turnover under €35,000 for 2026? This determines VAT registration and price display wording. Consult a **Maltese accountant** to confirm your eligibility.

2. **Sole trader registration name:** If you've registered a trading name with Jobsplus (e.g., "Stagecraft Studio"), must that exact name appear on the website? Or your personal name? Confirm with **Jobsplus or a local business advisor**.

3. **Price display wording (ex-VAT case):** If not VAT-registered, what is the acceptable wording per Malta's Office for Fair Trading? The guidance recommends "VAT not applicable" but confirm the phrasing with a **Maltese accountant or consumer protection advisor**.

4. **Cancellation terms for services:** Once you send a service proposal via WhatsApp, at what point is the right to withdraw excluded? This depends on when the service "commences" (e.g., site visit, first meeting, photography day). A **Maltese lawyer** should draft this clause if you want certainty.

5. **WhatsApp data routing & privacy:** Your privacy notice states data goes to WhatsApp/Meta. Confirm that Meta's privacy policy is transparent and accessible to users; if not, consider adding a link to [Meta's data policy](https://www.meta.com/privacy/). A **data protection lawyer** can advise if additional clauses are needed.

6. **Google Sheets backend (if used):** If your website is hosted on Google Sites or stores data in Google Sheets, check Meta's processor status. If Meta acts as a processor, ensure your privacy notice discloses this. Consult **IDPC guidance** or a data protection lawyer if uncertain.
