  • Large multichannel operations

publish + paywall
For: fast publishing + paid newsletters + membership, keeping 100% of revenue.
  • Creator newsletters with paid tiers
  • Paid blogs / multi-author publications
  • Owning your audience (vs Medium/Substack)
  • Selling physical products (it's not a shop)

€15–199/mo Pro · or self-hostEU host ✓ · 0% cut
WordPress vs Substack — the neighbours
WordPress: the deepest CMS — choose it over Ghost when you need plugins, complex content, or WooCommerce. Substack: built-in discovery + audience network, but takes a 10% cut — choose it for reach, Ghost to keep all revenue + branding.
Layer ⑤ · the money engine

Payment rails (not storefronts)

When a builder says "supports Stripe," this is what it means — the processor that actually moves the money. You don't pick these instead of a shop; your shop plugs into one. This is also the unavoidable card fee (~1.5–1.9% in the EU, not the US 2.9%).

Stripe
global default
For: the card engine under most builders — 195+ countries, every integration.
  • Any builder needing cards/subscriptions
  • Recurring billing, invoicing
  • Proven, reliable for Malta merchants
~2.9%+€0.30 online · 2.4%+€0.15 in-personMT ✓ · US parent
Mollie
EU-native
For: EU-native rails — data stays in the EU, cheaper local methods.
  • EU merchants wanting EU data residency
  • iDEAL / SEPA / Klarna at low flat fees
  • Avoiding a US-parent processor
1.8%+€0.25 card · SEPA €0.35EU ✓ (Amsterdam)

Platform cut AVOIDABLE

Software skimming a % per sale. 0% on Shopify-w/-Shopify-Payments, SumUp, Wix, Squarespace Core+, WooCommerce, Ecwid, Big Cartel, Ghost. 2% on Webflow's cheap ecom tier & BigCommerce's new open-payment fee. 10% on Substack.

Card processing UNAVOIDABLE

The rail's fee, on every platform. EU ~1.5–1.9% (Malta gets EU interchange caps), not the US "2.9%+30¢". Roughly equal everywhere — so it's the monthly fee + platform cut that actually separate the tools.

The EU / Malta lens

Data residency & your GDPR duties

Where customer data lives, and what's on you regardless of tool. Card numbers are always tokenised by the rail — you never store raw card data, keeping you out of serious PCI scope.

Stores customer data in…Tools
EU / your choiceShopify (EU-default) · SumUp · Mollie · Ghost · Framer · Hostinger · WooCommerce (EU host) · Ecwid (EU-friendly)
Multi-region / outsourcedWix · Lightspeed
US (relies on SCCs/DPF)Squarespace · Webflow · BigCommerce · Big Cartel · Stripe (US parent, but Malta-supported)

Your duties as controller — any tool, no exceptions

Pick a lawful basis (consent / contract / legitimate interest) · minimise data · set retention limits · honour access/erasure/export requests · report breaches to Malta's IDPC within 72h · sign the tool's DPA · add a real consent banner (Cookiebot / CookieHub / Iubenda — not the basic built-in) · publish a privacy + cookie policy. No tool is "GDPR-compliant out of the box" — they supply compliant infrastructure; the compliance is yours.

Your specific case (Malta shop, in-store + online, perishables, non-coder, hates a cut): the atlas lands on Shopify — the only fully-managed platform live in Malta with a real POS sharing one stock count, expiry apps, and 0% cut on Shopify Payments (≈ €156/mo all-in at €5k sales). Budget alternative: SumUp (≈ €107/mo, no native expiry → spreadsheet). Everything else is either not in Malta (Square) or a different layer's job.
