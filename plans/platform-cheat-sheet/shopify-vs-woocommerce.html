Shopify vs WooCommerce + Hostinger

Same end-goal — a shop that sells online + in-store — but two opposite deals. The whole comparison comes down to one trade: rent a finished product, or own a system you keep running.

Shopify
"I rent a finished product."

It works on day one. You touch the UI; Shopify runs the engine — hosting, security, updates, PCI — invisibly. Less control, nothing to maintain, nothing breaks on you.

WooCommerce + Hostinger
"I own a system."

Free software on a host you rent. Total control, 0% fees, your data — but you (or a dev) assemble it, update it, secure it, and fix it when it breaks.

The heart of it

Who does the work under the hood

Same shop on the surface. The difference is the invisible layer beneath — and who owns it. This is the "fine print & control vs just touch the UI" you asked about, made concrete.

▲SSL — auto via host (the easy bit)
▲Performance — caching, image optimisation, right host tier
▲If it breaks — you diagnose; no one's on call
Plain English

What "a dev" actually means here

You won't be writing code daily — but someone owns that technical layer above. On Shopify that someone is Shopify. On WooCommerce it's you or a hired hand.

"A dev" = a developer: a person who does the under-the-hood setup & upkeep WordPress needs. In practice that's either (a) you — learning WordPress admin, plugins and hosting panels — or (b) a freelancer/agency at ~€30–80/hr, or a monthly care plan ~€50–150/mo that quietly does updates, backups and security so the site doesn't rot. It's not "build an app from scratch" — it's "keep the plumbing running." Shopify simply includes that person in the subscription.

The upside · what you actually get

What you get out of each

Not features — outcomes. What each one buys you when you live with it.

What Shopify gets you

▶ a shop that runs itself
✓Your time back — zero maintenance, so all your hours go into selling, not sysadmin
✓Peace of mind — it won't get hacked or go down on you; 24/7 support owns problems

What WooCommerce + Hostinger gets you

▲Full ownership — you own the site, the data, the customer list; portable, no lock-in
▲0% cut forever + cheap base — software is free; only host + card fees
▲Unlimited customisation — make it do literally anything, no platform "you can't"
▲Full data access for AI — point any model/agent at your own database (fits your automations)

The honest summary of the payoff: Shopify buys you freedom from the work (time, reliability, simplicity) at a monthly price. WooCommerce buys you freedom of the work (ownership, control, no cut, custom AI) at the price of running it. You're choosing which freedom matters more.

For your case — Malta shop, perishables, non-coder, AI on top

The verdict

Shopify — for you. You said the platform must handle security for you and you want to run a shop, not sysadmin a website. Shopify is "just touch the UI": hosting, SSL, PCI, updates and backups are invisible, the till shares one stock count, expiry apps cover perishables, AI is built in, and it's 0% cut in Malta. ≈ €156/mo, nothing to maintain.
WooCommerce + Hostinger — only if you specifically need something Shopify won't allow (bespoke logic, deep custom AI on your own data, total ownership) and you'll either learn the upkeep or pay a dev ~€50–150/mo to own the plumbing. More power, more control, more responsibility.
One-line rule: pick Shopify if "the site must just work and never need me." Pick WooCommerce + Hostinger if "I want to own and customise everything, and I have hands (mine or hired) to keep it running."
