# Airbnb Audit — Stephanie's Mellieħa Flats (Triq l-Għerien)
*Public-side competitive audit · captured 29 June 2026 · benchmarked vs a 17-property Mellieħa 3-bed sample + AirDNA/Holidu market data + a 6-listing Superhost teardown. Currency: EUR.*

---

## The one-line verdict
A genuinely **good asset with strong demand, run like a part-time landlord rather than a revenue-optimised hospitality business.** Three flats, ~80% peak occupancy, a Guest-Favourite track record — yet priced below market, one rating-point short of Superhost, and selling a "room" where rivals sell a "holiday." The upside is unusually clean: most of the wins are **free or cheap**, and because she's already near-sellout, raising prices carries little risk.

**The single biggest mental shift:** she is competing on *price and availability* (cheap, always-blocked-because-booked) when she should be competing on *the view and the experience* (her actual edge). Everything below flows from that.

---

## What's actually good (protect, don't touch)
Grounded in 79 captured reviews + the photos — these are real, defensible strengths:
- **The view** — Mellieħa church + Għadira Bay + Gozo/Comino. The most-mentioned positive. Genuinely better than most comps.
- **Space** — "much bigger than the photos / than anticipated" recurs constantly. Her flats are large.
- **Cleanliness** — consistently praised (L3 sub-score 4.9). Not a problem to fix.
- **Hosting communication** — 100% response within an hour; guests love Stephanie + son Matteo's custom tourist brochure and in-person welcome.
- **The garden** (L2/L3) — lemon trees + BBQ; a real differentiator vs apartment-only rivals.
- **Free parking + no cleaning fee** — both are genuine competitive advantages (the market norm is no cleaning fee, and parking is gold in Malta). Keep and *advertise* them.

She does not have a quality problem. She has a **pricing, presentation and operations** problem.

## TIER 1 — the three things that move money or the rating (do these)

### 1. Raise prices. The occupancy data proves she's underpriced. *(Biggest € lever, near-zero risk)*
**Evidence (not opinion):**
- **Occupancy ~77–81% booked Jul–Sep** across all three flats (calendar capture) vs the Mellieħa market's 66%. Tagged "Rare find – usually booked." Peak weeks sold out months out.
- **Price is flat and below market:** ~€166–169/night all-in, and **identical on all three flats** — the brand-new 0-review L1, the Guest-Favourite L2, and the 122-review flagship L3 all priced the *same*.
- Market: shoulder median **€187** (she's −10–12%), peak median **€246** (she's ~−20–30%). Two independent methods (17-comp sample + AirDNA) agree.

**Near-sellout + below-market + token 1.7% weekly discount = textbook underpricing.** When you're ~80% full, the market is telling you the price is too low.

**Workable fix:**
- **Differentiate the three flats.** Stop pricing them identically. The 122-review flagship (L3) and Guest-Favourite (L2) have earned a premium; the new L1 should sit *below* them to launch (see Tier 1.3). Same-price-for-all leaves money on every booking.
- **Seasonal tiering**, not one flat rate: push Jul–Sep hard (she's already selling out — raise toward €220–240), ease in shoulder, discount winter for long stays.
- **Use a real dynamic-pricing tool (PriceLabs / Beyond), NOT Airbnb Smart Pricing** — Smart Pricing is documented to *under*price; the third-party tools lift revenue 15–25%.
- **Illustrative upside (assumptions stated):** ~200–220 booked nights/flat/yr × a conservative +€15–25/night blended × 3 flats ≈ **€9,000–€17,000/year**, weighted to peak where she's already full. Low downside because raising a near-sellout calendar barely dents occupancy.

### 2. Make AC genuinely free + switch to self-check-in. *(Fixes the 1-star root cause AND the Superhost blocker)*
This is one connected problem, and it's her **only serious reputational issue.**
- **The two 1-star reviews on L3 are both about the host entering the unit without consent** — and **one was explicitly to collect the AC coin money** while guests were out/asleep. The coin meter *forces* her to enter. Other reviews flag AC "payable, not specified" (expectation gap). [Evidence note: the listing now lists AC as an amenity; whether still coin-metered or recently changed, the perception and the entry incidents are real and recent (2025).]
- Separately, **all 6 top competitors offer self-check-in (lockbox); Stephanie does in-person only** — and she lives *across the street* and watches the units (a guest's own words). Guests experience this as surveillance ("watches vigilantly… from hanging a towel on a chair").

**Why it's the same fix:** kill the coin meter (fold AC into the nightly rate — every rival includes it) → she no longer needs to enter to collect coins. Add a **lockbox/self-check-in** → she's no longer physically present policing the flat. Together these turn "surveilled by the owner" into "independent holiday," remove the cause of every 1-star, and match the market on convenience. **Free to do, highest rating-impact change in the audit.**

### 3. Re-shoot and re-order the photos around the view. *(Biggest conversion lever)*
**Correction vs my first pass:** photo *count* is fine (≈37–61 per listing) — so this is **not** a "too few photos" problem. It's **quality, staging and ordering**:
- **All three lead with a plain living room; the view is buried at slots 9–13.** The listings are *named* after a view they don't show first. Competitors lead with their #1 asset (pool, view, styled kitchen) — that's why their click-through wins.
- **Even the genuine assets are shot badly:** the view photographed in flat midday haze *with power lines across the frame* and rooftop water-tanks in the foreground; the lemon-tree garden shot with patchy grass and no furniture.
- **Rooms are large but bare** — empty floor, dark dated wardrobes, repeated flower-canvas prints, no styling. This actively *undersells* the "bigger than the photos" strength.
- **Prime slots wasted** on a washing-machine close-up (L1), a street shot of the building block (L2), a communal stairwell and festa/fireworks promo images (L3).

**Workable fix (one afternoon + golden hour):** lead every listing with the best church-and-bay / sunset shot; light staging (made beds with layered white linen, a few cushions/rugs/plants, a set table, tidy the garden + add furniture); shoot the view at golden hour (deliver the advertised "sunset"); cut the dead-weight slots; curate to ~20–25 *strong* images per flat. The benchmark: ≥20 good photos → up to **+40% bookings** — she has the volume, she's missing the quality that earns that.

## TIER 2 — worth doing (real, lower individual impact)
- **Re-title around the USP.** Current titles are generic ("Apartment with an exceptional view."). Rivals front-load location + size + feature. Suggested (≤50 chars): L1 *"Mellieħa 3-Bed · Panoramic Bay & Church Views"*; L2 *"Sunset Views & Garden · 3-Bed Mellieħa"*; L3 *"Mellieħa 3-Bed · Sea Views, Garden & Parking."*
- **Rewrite description opening lines** to lead with view + space + garden + free parking, name the **Mellieħa draws** (Għadira Blue-Flag beach, Popeye Village, Comino/Blue-Lagoon & Gozo ferry, diving), and **honestly disclose** the AC arrangement + the stairs — pre-empting the two recurring gripes turns them from 1-star surprises into non-issues.
- **Beds:** "uncomfortable / too hard" recurs across L2 (×2) and L3 — *because it recurs across flats* it graduates from noise to a worthwhile cheap fix: mattress toppers + better pillows (~€100/flat). (Note: don't argue it in review replies — see below.)
- **Respond to reviews graciously, not defensively.** Her public replies justify/argue (storm, power-cut, "mattress is new orthopaedic," "detergent not included as guests prefer their own"). Future bookers read these. Acknowledge + show it's fixed.
- **Turn on Instant Book** (≈+20% bookings) and **flexible/moderate cancellation** — standard ranking/conversion levers she's not using.
- **Win Superhost** — she's *one rating-point away* (see below). Tier-1 fixes #2 + beds get her there.
- **Low-season strategy:** Malta winter demand is rising and it courts digital nomads — offer **30-day+ monthly rates with a dedicated-workspace amenity** to fill Nov–Mar (mind the new 90-consecutive-night cap).
- **Group/family angle:** she owns the whole building (Flats 1/3/5/6+). Market "book multiple flats for a large group" — a differentiator single-unit rivals can't match, and a route to direct repeat bookings ("will come back" recurs in reviews).

## TIER 3 — verify (compliance, do first as a checklist item)
- **Confirm the MTA short-let licence is current and the number is displayed outside the property** + 24/7 contact. Malta law requires it; unlicensed = fines to €23,000 + a 3-year ban attaching to the property, and a **new 2026 EU rule makes Airbnb share booking data** with authorities (enforcement tightening). As a 10-year business host she's *probably* compliant — but verify. (Note: Airbnb's in-app licence field is blank for *all* Malta listings, so that's not a red flag; the legal duty is physical signage.)
- Eco-tax: she charges €1.50 pp/night — which becomes the correct legal rate from **1 July 2026** (it's €0.50 until then). Non-issue from this week; just keep it on a separate invoice line, no VAT.

## What I am deliberately NOT recommending (and why)
*(So you can see what I filtered out — feedback should be workable, not a wish-list.)*
- **Don't retile / rip out the terrazzo floors** — authentic Maltese, not a defect. Style *around* them (rugs), don't replace them.
- **Don't try to fix the hill/stairs** — unfixable and capital-heavy. Just set expectations in the listing. (Note: L1 lists a lift as an amenity — if accurate, market L1 as the step-free option.)
- **Don't add a pool / major renovation** — not feasible for flats and unnecessary; the *view* is her equivalent hero USP. Sell that instead.
- **Don't compete on being the cheapest** — three comps already undercut her (€108–140); at ~80% occupancy a race to the bottom only destroys margin.
- **Don't deep-discount** — her calendar is full; the token weekly discount is fine, deeper isn't warranted.
- **Don't chase photo *quantity*** — she has 37–61 already; more weak photos won't help. Quality only.
- **Don't act on one-off gripes** (single "upstairs noise") — monitor, don't invest.
